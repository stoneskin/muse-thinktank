# The Human–AI Relationship

*Stone's notes from working with AI · first written 2026-10-08, ongoing*

---

## Core thesis: AI is an amplifier, not a replacement

Human value isn't in *what kind of work* you do, but in **how close you are to decisions and taste**.

---

## 1. AI eats the grunt work; humans keep the decisions

AI's value is in devouring the repetitive, mechanical, physical parts. What remains — judgment, decisions, taste — still belongs to humans.

But the boundary of "grunt work" keeps moving up. Writing unit tests was serious work a decade ago; now it's grunt work. What feels like "judgment work" today may be classified as grunt work in two years. The real safe zone isn't *what* you do, but **how close you are to decisions and accountability** — the person who can make the call and take the blame is the hardest to replace.

## 2. AI "creativity" is brute force; human creativity is taste

AI innovation often looks like releasing ten thousand ants into a maze — a few will find their way out. That's brute force producing miracles, not genuine flashes of insight. It's exhaustive search and recombination across a vast space, occasionally stumbling onto a path you haven't seen.

But much of human creativity is also combination and exhaustive search: a painter tries a hundred color schemes, an engineer tries dozens of designs. The difference isn't *whether* it's brute force, but **who decides which path is meaningful**. AI can exit the maze, but it doesn't know which exit is worth taking — taste and intent remain human.

## 3. From "writing it myself" to "steering AI": where the satisfaction moved

Satisfaction used to be **writing a program myself, shipping an app**.
Now it's **shipping more projects together with AI**.
And the deepest satisfaction is: **challenging the AI — making the project follow my intent and decisions, not being led by the nose.**

The human is the driver, not the passenger. AI should execute your direction, not give you directions.

But there's a trap: challenging AI requires keeping your own craft sharp. Stop writing code by hand for long enough, and one day you won't even notice when it drifts off course — steering becomes dozing in the passenger seat. **Keeping the ability to do it yourself is the precondition for steering.**

## 4. Cross-validation: checking AI with AI

In a domain you know well, you catch AI's mistakes immediately and correct them. In an unfamiliar domain, you're lost — whatever the AI says, goes.

The countermeasure: **use a different AI to review and correct** — preferably models from different families. Multi-model cross-validation covers your blind spots.

With a caveat: many models share training-data lineage, so their blind spots may be correlated — they can make the same mistake together. A real correction chain is: **different model families + your own judgment + a human expert at critical moments**. Model cross-checking filters rookie mistakes, not systematic hallucinations.

## 5. You must know what you want (2026-10-12)

AI-written code looks perfect but probably has bugs.

Reviewing with different models — especially different model families — fixes most bugs and shortcomings. But even with an AI think-tank review, **human testing and feedback remain indispensable** — you must know what you want, the concrete implementation approach, and the overall direction, to point out AI's errors and steer corrections.

---

*These five points form a complete worldview: AI amplifies human capability, but direction and taste are forever human.*
